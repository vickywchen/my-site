Microview: Analog Microscope Camera

for 4.053 — Objects and Interaction

with Kayla Han

The device features a wooden outer casing inspired by the aesthetic of antique brass-and-wood microscopes, combined with 3D-printed components. The door allows easy internal access to swap slides and swap sensitive media without disturbing the light seal.

Seeing beyond the microscopic.

Inspired by the mechanisms of a microscope, this “camera” captures a magnified image of a small object on cyanotype. We explore tiny worlds, not just how they look, but the mystery beyond visibility itself.

Inside, the rack-and-pinion enables focus adjustments via a side-mounted knob that shifts the lens vertically. A dedicated slot secures standard specimen slides directly within the focal plane.

CAD of the interior mechanisms

We created multiple test prints and tweaks to get the gears fitting smoothly in real life compared to the 3D model. On the printmaking side, we trialed different UV light intensities and exposure times to produce crisp cyanotype prints.

To “capture” the image, a UV light source shines downward through the specimen slide and focusing lens. The magnified image is projected directly onto light-sensitive cyanotype paper placed at the base.
