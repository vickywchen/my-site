Pantry to Plate: Recipe Camera

for 4.031 — Objects and Interaction

with Kayla Han and Jocelyn Pak

How can we reimagine the digital camera?

Pantry to Plate is a kitchen assistant that turns a photo of your ingredients into a complete recipe.

The user experience is designed around physical interaction, inspired by common actions in cooking — pressing a timer, shaking a pan, twisting a lid. A side-mounted button captures an image of the ingredients, shaking the device generates a different recipe, and twisting the top resets the system to start over.

The enclosure combines a 3D-printed PLA body with a wooden top cap, designed with a slide-fit mechanism for easy assembly and disassembly.

Exploded rendering of the device

On the hardware side, the device is built around an Arduino Uno, with inputs from physical controls such as a potentiometer for the turning mechanism and tilt switches to detect the shaking triggers.

A camera loop continuously streams frames using OpenCV, while a serial connection to the Arduino listens for physical inputs to trigger state transitions. When a capture event occurs, the system sends the image to a vision-language model to extract the ingredients. That output is then passed into a second model that generates a recipe, including steps and a prompt for image generation. A diffusion model is used to synthesize a corresponding food image. View my code repository here.

The UI is designed to be easy to navigate without on-screen controls, since all interaction happens through physical interactions.
